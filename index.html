S 01 Foundation

Scent with purpose.

Neurora LABS makes neuroscience-driven scent — formats built around the olfactory pathways that govern four states of the human nervous system. The work is not perfume. It is not aromatherapy. It is not wellness as a category. It is a small set of precisely engineered tools for shifting state, sold to people who want their tools to do work.

The system in this book is the typographic, chromatic, and verbal expression of that posture. Every rule that follows is downstream of one premise: the brand should look and read the way the product behaves — exact, restrained, useful, and warm where warmth earns its place.

§ 01 · Premise
Olfaction is the shortest path to state.

The olfactory bulb is the only sense wired directly into the limbic system. No thalamic relay, no cognitive filter. A scent reaches the structures that govern arousal, memory, and autonomic tone in under four hundred milliseconds.

§ 02 · Discipline
Built on science. Made for owning your state.

Each format targets a specific shift — sympathetic to parasympathetic, low arousal to focused alertness — and is delivered in the dose, duration, and surface area appropriate to the context of use.

§ 03 · Posture
Educated peer, not guru.

The brand addresses informed adults — curious about how their nervous system works, sceptical of overclaim, and looking for tools rather than rituals. It does not explain emotions back to people, does not promise transformation, and does not borrow the vocabulary of wellness, ritual marketing, or self-help.

§ 04 · Format
Three formats, four states.

Reed Diffuser 100 ml, Fabric Spray 30 ml, Roll-On 10 ml — each tuned to the spatial and temporal envelope of the state it serves. Sleep is the hero. The other three orbit it.

S 02 Mission · Vision · Beliefs

The anchors. Why we exist, where we're going, what we hold.

Three short anchors. They sit above every other rule in this book — when a brief, a campaign, or a product decision conflicts with one of them, the anchor wins. Type and palette are how neurora looks; these are why it exists.

The mission is what we do. The vision is the world we are working toward. The beliefs are the convictions that decide how we behave when no rule covers the case.

§ 01 · MissionWhy we exist

We translate the science of scent into tools for owning your state.

Neurora LABS exists to close the distance between olfactory neuroscience and everyday human use — making the measured effects of scent on the nervous system accessible, precise, and held to a standard.

The work is not perfume, not aromatherapy, not wellness. It is a small set of engineered instruments, each built around a specific autonomic shift, and delivered in the format that the context of use requires.

§ 02 · VisionWhere we're going

A world where shaping your state is everyday, evidence-based, and yours.

We work toward a future in which functional scent is a normal part of how people focus, recover, and rest — as deliberate as what they eat, as intentional as how they move — and remains in their own hands rather than the hands of an industry.

The vision is not a market. It is a category that does not yet fully exist: scent treated as instrumentation, sold to informed adults.

§ 03 · Core beliefs — Six convictions What we hold when the rules run out.
B · 01

Scent is one of the most powerful levers of human experience.

The olfactory system is the only sense wired directly into the limbic structures that govern arousal, emotion, and memory. We design around that pathway with intention — never as decoration, never by accident.

B · 02

The four states are biological, not poetic.

Activate, Focus, Calm, Sleep are autonomic and neuromodulatory states with measurable signatures. Our work is to identify the compounds, pathways, and dosages that influence each one with predictable effect — and to publish the reasoning when we do.

B · 03

You deserve to know why it works.

We cite our references. We name our actives. We explain the mechanism. If a claim cannot be sourced, the claim is not made. An informed customer is the only kind of customer this brand is built for.

B · 04

Nature, refined by science.

We work with organically sourced, naturally derived essential oils as our active ingredients — chosen for evidence, not romance. Science is how we study, formulate, and dose them so each compound delivers a measurable effect within a defined envelope of use.

B · 05

Objects that earn daily use.

An instrument that holds up to repeated use rewards it. The roll-on that lives in a pocket. The bottle that weighs in the hand. The reed that sits on the nightstand for eight weeks. The product is not a ritual — it is a tool that becomes part of how a person already lives.

B · 06

We change the world by changing ourselves.

Better-rested, more-focused, calmer, more-present people build better work and better lives around them. The brand's contribution begins at the smallest scale — one person, one evening, one shift from alert to at rest — and accumulates from there.

S 04 Voice

Precise. Calm. Educated peer.

The voice carries calm authority. It addresses the reader as an equal — informed, curious, sceptical of overclaim — and does the cognitive work itself rather than asking the reader to do it. It uses ordinary words for ordinary things and reaches for the technical term only when the technical term is the right one.

The voice is allowed warmth. It is not allowed sentimentality, mysticism, urgency, or the language of self-improvement.

§ 01 · Register
Exact.

Concrete units. Specific structures. "400 milliseconds," not "almost instantly." "Parasympathetic shift," not "deep relaxation." Precision is a form of respect.

§ 02 · Register
Quiet.

The product carries the volume. The copy does not need to perform. Short sentences. No exclamation, no rhetorical underline. Confidence reads as composure.

§ 03 · Register
Warm where it earns it.

Italic for the human moment — own your night, permitted, not made. Used sparingly. The serif italic is the brand's smile, not its default expression.

S 05 Typography

Humanist pharmaceutical. Three voices, one system.

Three typefaces, each with a single, defended job. The serif carries warmth and the spoken voice. The sans carries function — labels, navigation, button text, anything that needs to be read at a glance. The mono carries data, and only data — numbers, units, codes, specifications.

The discipline is the system. Mono is never used for headlines. Serif is never used for UI labels. Sans never sets editorial body. The visible separation between the three is what makes neurora read as pharmaceutical-precise rather than coder-aesthetic or wellness-soft.

R · 01
Mono is for data, full stop. Numbers, units (ml, ms, wk), prices, codes, section markers, lot numbers. Never headlines, never running copy, never word labels.
R · 02
Italic is the spoken moment. One italic emphasis per line, maximum. Two italic words in a single headline is a tell that the line is overworking.
R · 03
Caps belong to sans. All-caps treatments use Inter Tight 500 with +0.22 to +0.32em tracking. Newsreader is never set in all-caps.
R · 04
Optical sizes are not optional. Newsreader is a variable face — use opsz 72 at display, opsz 18 at editorial body, opsz 14 at small print. The face is designed for it.
R · 05
Tracking pulls tight at scale. Display sizes use negative tracking (−0.024em to −0.032em). Body stays at default. UI caps go positive (+0.22 to +0.32em). Consistency over personal taste.
R · 06
The hairline is 0.5 px. Every divider in the system — between cells, under labels, around frames — is a 0.5 px line in ink at 12% opacity. Heavier rules undo the pharmaceutical register.
S 06 Color

Paper, ink, and four pigments.

The system is paper-native. Paper is the ground, Navy is the ink. Warm grey provides the only secondary surface — spec strips, deep footers, instrument panels. The four state pigments are oxide colors, not jewelry colors — burnt sienna, viridian, slate, navy. They appear in marks, accents, and packaging foil. They never fill body backgrounds or act as decoration.

Colors are named for the state they belong to. Hex values are absolute. The brand defends the difference between Activate and gold, between Focus and tech-green, between Calm and pastel blue. The four pigments must read as mineral on the eye.

What the system does

  • +Paper is the default ground, not Navy. The system reads as a document, not as a hero image. Navy hero panels are used for closing statements and Sleep-only contexts.
  • +State pigments are pigments — oxide, mineral, glass — never neon, never gradient, never glossy. They appear as flat color in marks, packaging caps, and accent rules.
  • +The four states sit in daily-biological order: Activate (morning), Focus (mid-day), Calm (evening), Sleep (night). This order is preserved in every navigation, listing, and label.
  • +Soft tints exist only for spec strips — never for state-themed backgrounds. A Calm page does not turn slate-blue. The Calm pigment marks the state from the corner; the page stays paper.

What the system rejects

  • −Gold, copper as ornament, metallic foils that read as perfume packaging. Activate is iron oxide — earth pigment — not jewelry. If it shines, it is wrong.
  • −Sage, mint, eucalyptus green. Focus is a deep clinical viridian, not the wellness-pharmacy green that has become category default since 2020.
  • −Pastels of any kind. Calm is a mineral dusk, not a baby blue. Soft tints are bone-paper grey with a hint of pigment, never confectionary.
  • −Gradients between states. The four are discrete tools, not a spectrum. They are presented as four flat tiles, never as a fade.
S 07 States

Four tools. One day.

Each state is a distinct format, target, and claim — never a mood. The four sit in the order the body needs them: Activate at waking, Focus through working hours, Calm at the transition out of the day, Sleep at night. This is the order the brand uses in every nav bar, listing, and label. Reordering them — alphabetical, by popularity, by SKU code — is a category error.

S 08 Wordmark

A serif word. Lowercase, set tight.

The wordmark is the single word neurora, set in Newsreader 400 at display optical size, all lowercase, tracking −0.014em. The serif itself is the differentiator — no sans wordmark in the category does this work. The lab descriptor LABS is held in Inter Tight 500, always uppercase, separated from the mark by a 0.5px hairline. The mark and the descriptor are equals, not parent and child.

Two lockups are sanctioned: a stacked primary (mark above, LABS as widely-tracked descriptor below, used at hero and packaging scale) and an inline compact (LABS to the right of the mark, used for nav bars, business cards, footers, and email signatures). A custom-drawn wordmark is the long-term goal; until that exists, Newsreader at display sizes is the standard. The mark is never bolded, never italicized, never set in caps, never reproduced in mono.

S 09 Application

How neurora appears on surface.

The system meets the world on four primary surfaces: the packaging label (the most defended surface — it carries the product alone, with no context), the site hero (the brand's introduction at scale), the editorial / journal page (where the brand earns its authority through writing), and stationery (where the brand introduces itself as a sender, not a seller).

Each surface follows the same rules — Paper ground, Navy ink, Newsreader for voice, Inter Tight for function, JetBrains Mono for data, a 0.5px hairline as the only divider. What changes is density and pacing. The label is information-dense and exact. The hero is spacious and quiet. The editorial page is long-form and serif-led. Stationery is sparse — almost all margin.

Application rules

The hairline carries the architecture. Every surface is composed as a grid of compartments separated by 0.5px hairlines in ink at 12% opacity. The hairline replaces the box, the card, the shadow. The whole system is one ruled page.

Density signals register. Packaging and editorial are dense — the system tolerates and rewards information-richness. Hero surfaces and stationery are sparse — they earn their authority through restraint. The same elements; different pacing.

State pigments mark, they do not flood. A state-themed surface introduces its pigment as a 6–8 px dot, a hairline accent, a foil cap, or a small filled block. It never replaces Paper as the page background. The brand stays paper-native at every scale.

State marks defend their own contrast. On a paper label against a paper ground, the state pigment carries itself. On a dark or coloured bottle, the state mark must be enlarged, set on a paper block, or moved to a foil cap so it remains the at-a-glance differentiator between the four. A Sleep mark that disappears into a black bottle is not a mark.

Data is always set in mono. Across every surface — packaging, hero, editorial, stationery, email — anywhere a number, unit, code, or section marker appears, JetBrains Mono is the only acceptable face.

S 10 Photography direction

Register, not stock.

The system carries itself typographically, but the brand earns its full register only when the photography matches. Photography is treated the way data is treated — as evidence, not decoration. Four registers are commissioned; nothing outside them is used.

This section is a direction, not a moodboard. Until the photography exists, the brand operates on type and palette alone — the system is designed to hold its authority without imagery if it has to.

S 11 Don't

The boundary the brand defends.

These are not preferences. These are the failures the system was built to prevent — the places where neurora would lose category separation, scientific credibility, or its claim to seriousness. Every "don't" below has been seen in the category. None of them belongs here.

D · 01
Don't use the words wellness, ritual, aromatherapy, journey, energy, vibes, mindful, self-care, heal, discover, or any variant. They belong to a different category.
D · 02
Don't use gold, metallic foil, or any finish that shines. Activate is iron oxide, not jewelry. If a surface catches light, it has lost the pharmaceutical register.
D · 03
Don't reorder the states. The order is Activate · Focus · Calm · Sleep — the biological order of the day. Alphabetical, SKU-coded, or popularity-based orderings are wrong.
D · 04
Don't set body copy in mono. Don't set headlines in mono. Mono is for data only — numbers, units, codes, section markers. Every other use erodes the rule.
D · 05
Don't flood the page in state color. Paper is the ground. Pigment marks the state from the corner, the cap, or the rule — never the background.
D · 06
Don't promise transformation. Don't use the second-person imperative as a sales tactic. "Own your night" is the brand voice; "transform your life" is not.
D · 07
Don't show faces. Photography is hands, materials, instruments, and rooms — the work, not the user. Aspirational lifestyle is a different category.
D · 08
Don't gradient the states. The four are discrete tools, not a colour-spectrum. They appear as four flat tiles, separated by hairlines, never as a fade.
D · 09
Don't borrow scientific authority without earning it. If a claim cannot be sourced, the claim is not made. The brand prefers a smaller, true sentence to a larger one that overstates.
D · 10
Don't make the system louder to make a piece work. If a layout needs more weight, the layout is wrong — the answer is more space, not more ink.
